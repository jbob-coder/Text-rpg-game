# THE GAME — World Population, Citizen Hierarchy and Social Status

Status: **ACTIVE / WORLD-SOCIAL STANDARD**

## 1. Purpose

This standard defines how populations, citizen status, social hierarchy, institutional privilege, discrimination, NPC density, migration and local social structure are represented spatially.

## 2. Population record

Each settlement/region may track:
- resident population band;
- transient population;
- age/household structure only if relevant;
- occupation distribution;
- institutional employment;
- security/military presence;
- education/training population;
- faction presence;
- wealth bands;
- housing bands;
- migration/displacement;
- named NPC anchors;
- supporting-NPC capacity;
- ambient crowd capacity.

Exact numbers remain optional until simulation requires them.

## 3. NPC persistence tiers

### Tier A — recurring canonical
Full persistent identity, memory, goals, relationships, schedule, assets.

### Tier B — named local
Persistent identity with reduced simulation detail.

### Tier C — generated/supporting persistent
Stable seed/ID, limited memory/goals, promotable.

### Tier D — ambient
Presentation-only unless promoted.

Promotion preserves prior identity/history when available.

## 4. Social status dimensions

Do not overload one variable.

Possible dimensions:
- citizenship/residency;
- legal status;
- wealth;
- occupation;
- education/training;
- institutional rank;
- military/security rank;
- faction standing;
- professional license;
- criminal/legal status;
- lineage/culture/species only where final canon establishes them;
- public reputation.

## 5. Access effects

Status may affect:
- areas/buildings;
- jobs;
- prices only through economy policy;
- services;
- dialogue;
- law/security response;
- permits/equipment legality;
- education/training;
- political participation;
- faction opportunities.

Effects must be authored/systemic and player-visible where appropriate.

## 6. Discrimination/prejudice model

Separate:
- law;
- institution;
- local culture;
- faction doctrine;
- individual belief;
- economic incentive;
- propaganda;
- conflict pressure.

A fictional prejudice system must allow variation and agency. Avoid “everyone in group X hates group Y” global rules.

## 7. Racism/species prejudice

If final lore contains multiple human groups/species/cultures and discrimination:
- define the fictional groups;
- historical causes;
- region variation;
- legal status;
- institutional practices;
- individual variation;
- resistance/mobility;
- player agency;
- consequences.

Do not infer real-world protected traits onto the user/player. This is worldbuilding for fictional populations.

## 8. NPC schedule density

Population simulation should use budgets:
- high-detail near player/important events;
- medium detail for named off-screen NPCs;
- aggregate updates for distant ordinary populations.

The base game should remain deterministic/saveable without hosted AI.

## 9. Rumor and knowledge

Population systems may propagate:
- public news;
- faction information;
- rumors;
- witnessed player actions.

Private/hidden knowledge stays inside authoritative social state until legitimately propagated.

## 10. Gate Twelve status

Tamsin is currently the main named recurring NPC in the vertical slice.

Gate Twelve's full civilian/workforce population model is not established. Depot Plaza, Workshop Row and municipal institutions provide anchors for future population records but do not justify invented demographic totals.
