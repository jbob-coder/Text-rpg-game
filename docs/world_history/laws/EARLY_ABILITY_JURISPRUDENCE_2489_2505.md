## Purpose

Records durable legal principles emerging before the later 2514 public-safety model.

These are representative trends, not one global constitution.

## PRINCIPLE_INNATE_STATUS_NOT_CONTRABAND

In several influential jurisdictions, courts distinguish an innate ability from a possessed weapon or regulated object.

Meaning:
A person cannot be treated as possessing contraband merely because the ability exists in their body.

This does not prevent regulation of use.

## PRINCIPLE_INTENT_RECKLESSNESS_FORESEEABILITY

Ability-related liability increasingly distinguishes:
- intentional use;
- reckless use;
- negligent failure to control a known hazard;
- involuntary manifestation.

Exact standards differ by jurisdiction.

## PRINCIPLE_PROFESSIONAL_USE_LICENSING

Professions involving deliberate high-risk ability use may require:
- training;
- medical clearance;
- insurance;
- competency demonstration;
- incident reporting.

## PRINCIPLE_EMERGENCY_SELF_DEFENSE

Some jurisdictions recognize ability use under ordinary or modified self-defense rules.

Disputes remain over proportionality where an ability's effects are difficult to measure.

## PRINCIPLE_SCHOOL_ACCOMMODATION_AND_SAFETY

Schools increasingly owe duties to:
- respond to manifestation safely;
- avoid automatic expulsion for mere status;
- create individualized control/safety plans where practical.

More coercive jurisdictions retain broader exclusion powers.

## PRINCIPLE_MEDICAL_PRIVACY_LIMITS

Medical ability information is protected to varying degrees, but exceptions commonly include:
- imminent danger;
- defined licensed occupations;
- court order;
- military/security service under specific law.
