# ABILITY_REGISTRATION_RIGHTS_CONFLICT_2475_2488

Live-game clock effect: NONE

## Problem

After Veinfall, governments possess strong public-safety reasons to know about some dangerous manifestations, but compulsory universal registration creates civil-rights, medical-privacy, and hereditary-surveillance risks.

No global law is created in this period.

## Three representative models

### MODEL_A_DANGEROUS_USE_REGISTRATION
Registration triggered by:
- harmful public use;
- professional use;
- criminal conviction involving ability;
- court-certified high public risk.

Strength:
narrower intrusion.

Weakness:
authorities may learn about dangerous capabilities only after an incident.

### MODEL_B_CONFIRMED_USER_REGISTRATION
All medically/security-confirmed ability users must register.

Strength:
broad emergency planning.

Weakness:
creates large government identity database and discrimination risk.

### MODEL_C_MEDICAL_PRIVACY_WITH_WARRANT
Ability information treated primarily as protected medical data except:
- imminent danger;
- defined licensed occupations;
- judicial authorization.

Strength:
stronger privacy.

Weakness:
security institutions consider it too restrictive.

## Rights conflicts

Recurring disputes:
- employment discrimination;
- school placement;
- insurance;
- military service;
- family-lineage records;
- involuntary manifestation liability;
- self-defense;
- ability suppression/restraint;
- search and seizure;
- medical confidentiality.

## Historical significance

The legal conflict establishes a lasting principle:

Ability regulation is not equivalent to crystal regulation.

A crystal can be property.

An innate ability is part of a person.

Later law repeatedly struggles with that distinction.
