## Core identity

Homunculi are sapient, humanlike beings whose bodies combine humanoid morphology with beast-derived and crystal-adapted biology.

They are not mindless monsters, summons, pets, or disposable encounter units. A homunculus can possess:
- individual personality;
- long-term memory;
- language;
- family and social bonds;
- fear, ambition, resentment, affection, loyalty, ideology, and trauma;
- tactical and strategic reasoning;
- culture and transmitted knowledge;
- independent goals.

They may look close to human at a distance while retaining clearly nonhuman traits depending on lineage: unusual eyes, dermal structures, claws, altered musculature, horns, tails, fur/scales, crystalized tissues, specialized sensory organs, or other stable beast-derived anatomy.

## Origin

### CLASSIFIED_TRUE / DRAFT_CANON

Homunculi originated during the Crystal Era from human attempts to produce controllable crystal-adapted humanoid labor and combat organisms.

The first viable programs combined:
- human-compatible developmental scaffolds;
- selected beast-derived biological traits;
- crystal-mediated tissue stabilization;
- engineered neural development intended to support complex task learning.

The project succeeded too well. Mature homunculi were not merely trainable organisms. They demonstrated full sapience, self-preservation, social identity, and the ability to understand their own legal captivity.

Early generations were treated as manufactured assets rather than people.

Stable later lineages became capable of biological reproduction, allowing homunculus populations to continue independently of laboratories.

This origin remains partially classified in 2670. Public education presents a simplified account that emphasizes their military danger and subsequent rebellion.

## Historical conflict

### EVENT_HOMUNCULUS_REBELLION_2587_2601

Working date range: 2587–2601.
Status: DRAFT_CANON pending detailed era construction.

A network of homunculus labor compounds, military units, research populations, and escaped communities coordinated a large rebellion against human control.

The rebellion escalated from:
1. escapes and local sabotage;
2. seizure of facilities and weapons;
3. attacks on detention/research infrastructure;
4. formation of organized rebel territories and armed cells;
5. wider conflict with human governments and military forces.

At its height, the rebellion was regarded by many human societies as the most dangerous internal enemy humanity had yet faced.

The rebel movement was not ideologically uniform. Factions ranged from groups seeking legal recognition and freedom to militant organizations that considered human political control irreconcilable with homunculus survival.

Human civilians, soldiers, researchers, homunculi, and other populations all suffered during the conflict. Exact casualty figures remain to be designed and must not be invented during narration before the historical record is written.

### Public interpretation

PUBLIC_BELIEF in most human jurisdictions:
- humanity created or tolerated the homunculi;
- homunculi betrayed humanity;
- the rebellion nearly caused catastrophic internal collapse;
- permanent control is therefore treated as necessary for human security.

### Classified/true complication

CLASSIFIED_TRUE:
- coercive ownership, forced experimentation, restricted reproduction, disposability doctrines, and attempted termination/recall programs were major causal factors in the rebellion;
- some human authorities knew the homunculi were fully sapient before granting them any meaningful legal recognition;
- neither the human nor homunculus side acted as a single unified moral bloc.

This distinction is important for historical causality without erasing the reality that homunculus rebel forces committed serious violence against humanity.

## Defeat and present legal status

### LAW_HOMUNCULUS_CUSTODIAL_ORDER_2602

After organized rebel power was defeated, dominant human authorities adopted a legal regime that classified surviving homunculi and most descendants as controlled nonhuman assets rather than citizens.

By 2670, in the dominant human legal order:

- homunculi do not possess normal human citizenship privileges;
- they cannot vote or hold ordinary public office;
- independent land/property ownership is generally prohibited;
- unrestricted travel is prohibited;
- legal contracts are normally mediated through an owner, state institution, military authority, or licensed custodian;
- many are assigned, inherited, transferred, sold, leased, or requisitioned as forced labor;
- labor is compulsory rather than freely contracted;
- escape is treated as unlawful flight;
- organized armed resistance is treated as rebellion/terror activity;
- descendants generally inherit the legal classification unless a rare statutory exception applies.

This institution is slavery. In-world bureaucracies may call it custody, bonded service, strategic labor, or controlled-species management, but world-state documentation should not confuse those euphemisms with voluntary employment.

Some regulations restrict needless killing, mutilation, or destruction of registered homunculi, but these protections were historically framed around state order, asset preservation, and public safety rather than equal personhood.

## Present society

### STATUS_HOMUNCULUS_ENSLAVED_01

Most legally registered homunculi in major human-controlled areas fall under an enslaved or custodial status.

Possible assignments include:
- heavy industry;
- hazardous crystal handling;
- construction;
- mining;
- agriculture;
- logistics;
- domestic/service labor;
- military support;
- dangerous frontier work;
- research and technical work where a lineage has specialized capabilities.

Not every homunculus lives under identical conditions. Treatment depends on jurisdiction, owner/custodian, occupation, lineage, security classification, and personal history.

### Fugitive and free communities

Not all homunculi are under human control.

Persistent categories include:
- escaped individuals;
- hidden settlements;
- rebel remnants;
- smuggler-assisted fugitives;
- offworld enclaves;
- clandestine free communities;
- homunculi serving nonhuman powers;
- rare individuals with exceptional legal exemptions.

These populations do not automatically share one faction.

## Relationship to humanity

Historical hostility must not become an automatic NPC alignment rule.

A homunculus may:
- hate humanity;
- hate only particular institutions;
- fear humans without hating them;
- cooperate pragmatically;
- seek assimilation;
- seek emancipation;
- support renewed rebellion;
- reject rebel ideology;
- protect a human individual;
- betray another homunculus;
- participate in human institutions under coercion or genuine attachment.

Likewise, humans differ in their attitudes toward homunculi.

The simulation must track individual knowledge, memory, ideology, relationships, incentives, fear, and material circumstances rather than assigning species-wide behavior.

## Biology and ability interaction

Homunculi are living biological organisms, not magical constructs.

Baseline design:
- they require food, water, rest, oxygen, and medical treatment appropriate to lineage;
- they can be injured, poisoned, diseased, exhausted, and killed;
- many lineages have beast-derived physical or sensory traits;
- some lineages possess crystal-adapted organs or tissues;
- some may express ability-like biological phenomena;
- homunculus traits are not automatically equivalent to natural human abilities;
- compatibility with human ability-transfer systems, including Steal, remains UNKNOWN until specifically defined.

Do not assume Jack's Steal can copy/remove a homunculus biological trait unless a later authoritative mechanic explicitly establishes that interaction.
