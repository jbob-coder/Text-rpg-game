## Purpose

This registry describes the institutional layer between the first surviving repeatable proof in 1896 and the multinational information exchange of 2228.

The central historical fact is fragmentation.

States and institutions increasingly know that genuine human anomalies exist, but they do not share one theory, one archive, one ethical standard, one vocabulary, or one strategic objective.

## ORG_ANOMALOUS_PHYSIOLOGY_UNIT_001

Existing root organization.
Established: 1896
Primary domain: medical/scientific verification
Historical role:
- preserves the first high-confidence repeatability dataset;
- develops basic fraud-control and physiological-observation standards;
- becomes a source of personnel and methods for later programs;
- never controls global anomaly research.

## ORG_NORTHMERE_ARCHIVAL_BUREAU_001

Established: 1904
Primary site: SITE_NORTHMERE_SECURE_ARCHIVE_1904
Primary domain: records continuity
Public cover: hydrological and engineering records

Functions:
- receives sealed copies of unusual medical/military cases;
- assigns cross-reference numbers without declaring a unified cause;
- maintains chain-of-custody standards;
- stores some originals too politically sensitive for ordinary ministry archives.

Weaknesses:
- depends on states voluntarily forwarding records;
- classification rules are inconsistent;
- some agencies intentionally send incomplete versions;
- archive staff can preserve evidence without understanding it.

## ORG_BRAEWOOD_EVALUATION_SECTION_001

Established: 1918
Primary site: SITE_BRAEWOOD_FIELD_EVALUATION_RANGE_1918
Primary domain: controlled field testing / military-medical evaluation

Functions:
- tests high-risk manifestations under controlled distance and terrain;
- compares medical stress response with observable external effect;
- develops early protective-distance guidance.

Historical abuses:
- coercive recruitment in wartime;
- subjects kept under security detention;
- medical consent standards below later norms;
- failure to distinguish national-security urgency from human experimentation.

Legacy:
Later doctrine inherits Braewood's safety geometry while formally repudiating many of its human-subject practices.

## ORG_RAVELIN_MEDICAL_VARIANCE_CENTER_001

Established: 1946
Primary site: SITE_RAVELIN_MEDICAL_VARIANCE_CENTER_1946
Primary domain: differential diagnosis / long-term observation

Functions:
- distinguishes disease, inherited variation, trauma, fraud, and unexplained ability;
- maintains longitudinal health records;
- reduces the tendency to treat every unusual person as a weapon asset.

Reform history:

- 1981: independent medical review panel added;
- classified-security exemptions remain available.

## ORG_HELIX_SIGNAL_ANALYTICS_001

Established: 2029
Primary site: SITE_HELIX_SIGNAL_ANALYTICS_CENTER_2038
Primary domain: cross-database analytics

Functions:
- correlates field sensors, medical cases, family clustering, incident geography, and environmental anomalies;
- creates early machine-assisted pattern comparison;
- identifies that some case families share measurable signatures despite very different outward effects.

Failure mode:
Statistical correlation encourages several false unification theories. Better data increases confidence faster than understanding.

## ORG_KAIROU_HERITABLE_VARIANCE_PROGRAM_001

Established: 2079
Primary site: SITE_KAIROU_GENOMIC_OBSERVATORY_2088
Primary domain: genetics / epigenetics / family studies

Findings:
- familial clustering is real;
- exact ability expression does not follow simple one-gene inheritance;
- non-manifesting relatives can carry measurable compatibility markers;
- environmental/developmental factors matter.

Ethical controversy:
- lineage databases become attractive to military and intelligence customers;
- several jurisdictions restrict secondary use;
- black-market family-risk reports emerge.

## ORG_MERIDIAN_PREPARATORY_FORUM_001

Established: 2187
Successor event: EVENT_MERIDIAN_INFORMATION_EXCHANGE_2228
Primary domain: confidential international comparison

Purpose:
- determine whether cases reported by rival states are comparable at all;
- create neutral terminology;
- reduce the risk that an unexplained ability incident is misread as a foreign weapon program.

Structure:
- no power to compel disclosure;
- sanitized case summaries only;
- source identity often withheld;
- strategic cases routinely excluded.

Historical success:
By 2228, the forum proves that genuine anomalous manifestations occur across unrelated populations and are not owned by one state, lineage, laboratory, or ideology.

## Institutional competition model

Common reasons programs conceal data from one another:
- fear of espionage;
- military advantage;
- medical privacy;
- political embarrassment;
- protection of subjects;
- desire for funding;
- ideological disagreement;
- bureaucratic rivalry;
- fear that another agency will seize jurisdiction.

This fragmentation is a major reason humanity reaches the 23rd century without a complete model.

## Four broad program cultures

### MODEL_A — ARCHIVAL_SECURITY
Priority:
Preserve evidence, restrict access, prevent loss.

Strength:
Long memory.

Weakness:
Can hoard data without producing understanding.

### MODEL_B — MILITARY_EVALUATION
Priority:
Threat, utility, controllability, field performance.

Strength:
Operational measurement.

Weakness:
Ethical abuse and overemphasis on weapon potential.

### MODEL_C — CLINICAL_VARIANCE
Priority:
Health, diagnosis, long-term function.

Strength:
Human-centered longitudinal data.

Weakness:
May under-share security-relevant information.

### MODEL_D — ANALYTIC_COMPARISON
Priority:
Pattern detection across institutions and borders.

Strength:
Finds correlations invisible to isolated programs.

Weakness:
Correlations can be mistaken for causal explanation.

## Public cover and ordinary life

Through most of 1896–2228:
- ordinary people do not live under public ability law;
- schools do not teach a standard ability taxonomy;
- hospitals do not routinely advertise anomaly clinics;
- most genuine cases are misdiagnosed, hidden, privately managed, or absorbed into classified systems;
- public claims remain mixed with fraud, religion, entertainment, rumor, and rare unexplained incidents.

The hidden-program world is small relative to ordinary civilization.

That asymmetry matters: secrecy survives because the phenomenon remains rare, not because one organization controls society.
