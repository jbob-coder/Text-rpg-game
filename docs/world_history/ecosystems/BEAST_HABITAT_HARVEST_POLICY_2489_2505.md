# BEAST_HABITAT_HARVEST_POLICY_2489_2505

Status: ACTIVE_ECOLOGICAL_POLICY_FRAMEWORK
Authority: ERA_030_EARLY_CRYSTAL_2473_2505
Live-game clock effect: NONE

## Problem

By the 2490s, some crystal-adapted organisms are:
- dangerous;
- scientifically valuable;
- ecologically important;
- economically attractive.

A pure extermination policy and unrestricted harvest are both unsustainable.

## Habitat categories

### HABITAT_PROTECTED_RESEARCH
Entry by permit.
Primary goal:
long-term ecological observation.

### HABITAT_BUFFER_MANAGED
Human settlement and anomalous ecology separated by controlled corridors, fencing, deterrence, and monitoring.

### HABITAT_HARVEST_RESTRICTED
Limited removal under quota/research permit.

### HABITAT_ERADICATION_PRIORITY
Used where a species/population creates intolerable risk and ecological containment is impractical.

## Early harvest rule

Before 2505, harvest is not yet the mature commercial beast-core industry.

Permitted recovery may include:
- dangerous animal destroyed during response;
- scheduled research removal;
- population-control cull;
- naturally dead specimen;
- limited quota harvest.

Required records increasingly include:
- site;
- estimated population;
- sex/age class if known;
- cause of death;
- tissue condition;
- crystal condition;
- ecological effect.

## Pressure effects

High-value crystal-bearing organisms attract poaching.

Consequences:
- breeding adults preferentially removed;
- habitat intrusion increases;
- response-team risk increases;
- black-market biological material expands;
- researchers begin advocating protected breeding populations.
