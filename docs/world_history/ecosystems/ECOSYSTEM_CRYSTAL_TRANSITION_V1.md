## Purpose

This file defines how every Crystal Era ecosystem must be reconstructed so beasts are organisms inside food webs rather than encounter tables.

## Origin classes

### ECO-NATIVE
Ordinary terrestrial lineage with no stable crystal adaptation.

### ECO-ALTERED
Terrestrial lineage altered by crystal exposure but still clearly descended from the original species.

### ECO-CRYSTALIZED
Lineage with stable heritable crystal-associated biology.

### ECO-OFFWORLD
Lineage introduced through portal transfer.

### ECO-HYBRID
Population created through interbreeding, symbiosis, gene transfer, engineered intervention, or long-term mixed ecology.

### ECO-ENGINEERED
Human or nonhuman intentionally engineered organism.

## Crystal ecology mechanisms

Possible effects include:
- increased energy storage;
- altered metabolism;
- unusual thermal tolerance;
- electro/field sensing;
- toxin resistance;
- reinforced tissue;
- enhanced regeneration;
- new symbioses;
- reproductive failure;
- malignant growth;
- behavioral instability;
- increased metabolic cost.

Every advantage should carry biological cost or constraint unless evidence establishes otherwise.

## Beast definition

"Beast" is a social/operational term, not a rigorous biological kingdom.

A jurisdiction may call an organism a beast when it:
- exhibits stable crystal-associated traits;
- poses unusual hazard;
- yields regulated crystal material;
- descends from a recognized beast lineage;
- is classified by law as controlled anomalous fauna.

Therefore:
- not every dangerous animal is a beast;
- not every beast is hostile;
- not every beast is large;
- some beasts can be domesticated;
- some may be ecologically essential.

## Harvest-pressure model

Commercial crystal extraction changes evolution.

Likely consequences:
- hunters preferentially remove high-yield adults;
- populations shift toward earlier reproduction;
- crystal size/output may decline where harvesting is intense;
- protected zones can preserve large specimens;
- illegal farms may select for fast crystal growth at health cost;
- predator removal can cause herbivore population explosions;
- harvesting one species can collapse another species that depends on it.

## Disease exchange

Portal-mediated ecology creates three major disease classes:

1. terrestrial pathogens infecting altered/offworld organisms;
2. offworld pathogens or analogues affecting terrestrial hosts;
3. crystal-mediated syndromes requiring an anomalous field rather than ordinary infection alone.

Quarantine history must be tracked separately from monster-control history.

## Gameplay consequence

A hunt can change world state.

Examples:
- overhunting reduces future crystal supply;
- removing a predator increases crop damage;
- destroying nests shifts migration;
- contaminated carcasses spread disease;
- preserving reproductive adults improves future populations;
- a rare beast sighting can imply a hidden portal/ecological corridor.

This framework should be referenced by encounter generation and regional world simulation.
