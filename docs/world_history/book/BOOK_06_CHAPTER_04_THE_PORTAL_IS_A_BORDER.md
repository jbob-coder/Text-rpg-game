By 2595, the portal was no longer only a machine.

It was a border.

People arrived with identities.

Cargo arrived with declarations.

Animals arrived with quarantine classifications.

Scientists arrived with permits.

Soldiers arrived with rules of engagement.

Settlers arrived with political expectations.

### 2597 — identity travels before the person

The portal network adopted interoperable identity and customs protocols.

The goal was not convenience.

It was accountability.

A traveler needed to be connected to:
- origin;
- destination;
- medical/quarantine status;
- legal permissions;
- employment or residency status.

Cargo required equivalent traceability.

The portal compressed physical distance.

It increased administrative density.

### Security without omniscience

Portal hubs became heavily monitored because one doorway could carry:
- a pathogen;
- contraband;
- sabotage;
- a dangerous organism;
- strategic technology.

But no security system knew everything.

False positives delayed innocent travelers.

Smugglers exploited human routines.

Local officials concealed politically inconvenient incidents.

Corporate operators sometimes underreported downtime.

The network was hardened.

It was not perfect.

### 2601 — redundancy becomes strategy

Major powers funded multi-hub redundancy so that one failed Earth terminal would not isolate multiple settlements.

Settlements also began stockpiling enough essentials to survive prolonged closure.

This changed their design:
- larger water storage;
- seed reserves;
- local fabrication;
- expanded clinics;
- emergency dormitories;
- redundant power.

The strongest frontier settlements became less dependent on daily portal traffic.

That made them more economically useful.

It also made them politically harder to control.

### 2603 — civilians outnumber expeditionary staff at selected nodes

Several settlement networks crossed a social threshold.

The population was no longer mainly:
scientists + security + extraction workers.

It included:
- children;
- teachers;
- merchants;
- repair trades;
- local administrators;
- long-term medical staff;
- farmers.

The frontier had become society.

### 2605 — from expeditions to interworld politics

By 2605, offworld settlement and resource networks were too large to be treated as exceptional projects.

Governments now had to manage:
- border law;
- taxation;
- land claims;
- environmental regulation;
- defense;
- settlement representation;
- corporate concessions;
- route access.

The era of Portal Expansion ended because portal travel had succeeded enough to create political problems larger than engineering.

Humanity was no longer only visiting other worlds.

It was beginning to live on them.

## End state

By 2605:
- artificial portals are repeatable but expensive;
- P3 controlled routes exist;
- a limited number of P4 strategic corridors exist;
- permanent research, extraction, agricultural, and civilian settlements exist;
- quarantine and rescue are network-level systems;
- frontier political identity exists;
- Earth access inequality remains severe;
- portal security is hardened but fallible.

No Kharvori contact is assumed here.
