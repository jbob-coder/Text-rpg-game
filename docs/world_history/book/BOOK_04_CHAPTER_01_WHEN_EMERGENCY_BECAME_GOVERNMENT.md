## Chapter 1 — When Emergency Became Government

By 2475, the emergency had lasted too long to keep calling every measure temporary.

Governments had built ministries out of task forces, careers out of emergency assignments, and permanent districts out of places that had once been marked "temporary shelter."

The first great political question of the Crystal Era was therefore not whether the world had changed.

It was which emergency powers would survive the emergency.

### The paperwork outlived the barricades

During Veinfall, a checkpoint could be justified by immediate danger.

By 2475, the same checkpoint needed:
- a legal authority;
- a budget;
- staffing;
- maintenance;
- appeal procedures;
- inspection standards;
- maps;
- recordkeeping;
- responsibility when something went wrong.

The improvisation of 2473 became bureaucracy.

This irritated almost everyone.

Residents wanted roads reopened.

Scientists wanted longer access windows.

Military commanders wanted authority to close areas quickly.

Farmers wanted compensation.

Businesses wanted predictable rules.

Ability users wanted protection from detention based only on fear.

Communities near persistent zones wanted more control over decisions made by distant capitals.

The Crystal Era did not produce one political answer.

It produced institutions built from those arguments.

### A refugee district becomes a town

Some people returned home.

Some discovered that home no longer existed in a legal sense.

A house inside a long-term exclusion zone could still be standing, but inaccessible.

A farm could remain physically intact while the water table made residence unsafe.

A neighborhood could be rebuilt only after months of biological monitoring.

Temporary districts therefore became permanent enough to need:
- schools;
- elected or appointed local councils;
- utility connections;
- postal addresses;
- property rules;
- policing;
- public transit;
- burial grounds;
- markets;
- zoning.

For children growing up there, "temporary housing" was simply home.

That generational fact mattered.

The first generation born after Veinfall would inherit the Crystal Era without remembering the world before it.

### Science needed buildings, not tents

The first months had produced enormous quantities of samples and almost no safe way to store them.

By 2475, governments and universities began constructing purpose-built crystal laboratories.

These facilities were designed around lessons learned painfully:
- samples needed independent containment;
- biological and geological material could not always share ventilation;
- storage geometry had to account for unknown field effects;
- power loss could not be allowed to disable every safety system;
- contaminated personnel needed separate exit routes;
- a specimen that looked inert could become active after environmental change.

A crystal laboratory therefore became a new architectural type.

Not quite hospital.

Not quite chemical plant.

Not quite military bunker.

It borrowed from all three.

### The birth of beast ecology

The word "beast" became more common before it became scientific.

Researchers resisted it at first because it grouped too many organisms together:
- altered terrestrial species;
- offworld organisms;
- stable crystal-adapted descendants;
- transient exposure victims.

But field teams needed practical categories.

A wolf with crystal-reinforced tissue was not handled like an ordinary wolf.

A harmless offworld grazer was not handled like a predator simply because it crossed a portal.

A scavenger that fed on crystal-rich carcasses could spread contamination without ever attacking a human.

By 2476–2477, universities and government agencies began creating dedicated anomaly-ecology programs.

Their central insight was simple:

If a creature reproduces, migrates, feeds, competes, and dies, then it belongs in an ecosystem even if its biology is extraordinary.

That principle prevented the world from becoming a permanent extermination campaign.

### Rights and registration

Ability registration became one of the most divisive political questions of the era.

Supporters argued registration was necessary for:
- emergency response;
- medical safety;
- dangerous-ability control;
- military readiness;
- insurance;
- investigation of ability-related crime.

Opponents argued that compulsory registration could become:
- hereditary surveillance;
- employment discrimination;
- forced recruitment;
- family-lineage tracking;
- preventive detention.

Different jurisdictions chose different compromises.

Some required registration only after dangerous public use.

Some required all confirmed users to register.

Some treated medical records as protected unless a court order applied.

Some allowed broad security access.

No global settlement emerged.

### The first training schools

The creature-response teams formed during extended instability discovered a limit to apprenticeship.

They needed repeatable training.

Early programs were not modern military academies.

They were specialist schools attached to:
- wildlife agencies;
- emergency-management institutes;
- military engineering commands;
- medical centers;
- quarantine authorities.

Students learned combinations that had not existed before Veinfall:
- field biology;
- hazard zoning;
- anatomy;
- specimen recovery;
- decontamination;
- small-unit movement;
- nonlethal capture;
- emergency medicine;
- crystal handling;
- ability-user coordination.

These schools are important because they are institutional ancestors of later academy systems.

They are not yet the modern Academy.

### Ordinary life resumes unevenly

By 2478, many people could go to work, school, restaurants, hospitals, and markets without confronting an anomaly every day.

That did not mean life had returned to normal.

Building entrances displayed hazard notices.

Public schools ran manifestation drills.

Food labels included regional origin certification.

Some trains carried sealed biological-compartment cars.

Hospital intake forms asked about crystal exposure.

Apartment leases near persistent zones included evacuation clauses.

Children traded rumors about exclusion areas the way earlier generations traded ghost stories.

The Crystal Era became ordinary by entering paperwork, architecture, education, and routine.

## What changed by chapter end

PUBLIC:
- permanent anomaly/crystal agencies exist in many states;
- some refugee districts have become legal municipalities or long-term administrative districts;
- beast ecology and ability medicine are recognized professional fields;
- ability-registration politics are mainstream.

CLASSIFIED:
- governments consolidate pre-Veinfall archives with Veinfall datasets;
- lineage information remains more extensive than public law admits in some jurisdictions;
- high-risk crystal storage standards exceed publicly described requirements.

TRUE:
- permanent institutions are being shaped as much by political fear and resource competition as by scientific need;
- the foundations of later Academy systems are emerging from separate training traditions, not one founding institution.
