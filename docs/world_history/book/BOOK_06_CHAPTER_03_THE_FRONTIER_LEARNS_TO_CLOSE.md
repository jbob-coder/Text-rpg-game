The first portal frontier had been built around opening routes.

After 2583, it was rebuilt around closing them safely.

### Quarantine architecture becomes permanent

Before the crisis, quarantine had often been treated as a facility attached to a portal.

Afterward, it became a network property.

Every major route needed:
- isolation capacity;
- diagnostic delay space;
- cargo holding;
- independent ventilation;
- dirty/clean personnel flow;
- emergency housing for stranded travelers.

That last requirement changed portal hubs permanently.

A closed route could trap hundreds or thousands of people on the wrong side.

### 2588 — corridor certification

Regulators created a route certification model based not only on whether a portal could open, but whether the entire corridor could fail safely.

A certified corridor now required:
- destination verification;
- return capacity;
- quarantine;
- rescue plan;
- power redundancy;
- emergency food/water;
- shutdown authority;
- event recording.

Portal engineering matured because failure became part of design.

### 2591 — rescue becomes mutual obligation

Settlements learned that no single route operator could guarantee rescue.

The Frontier Rescue Compact required participating hubs to maintain compatible emergency procedures and reserve transit capacity.

A competing corporation might become the only available evacuation route during a collapse.

That forced cooperation.

It also created arguments over cost.

Who paid when one network rescued another network's settlers?

### The politics of stranded people

A portal closure created strange legal situations.

A worker under Earth contract could be physically outside Earth jurisdiction.

A child could be born during a prolonged route shutdown.

A local council could order rationing against the wishes of an Earth employer.

A security commander could close a route that civilians wanted opened.

These were not abstract legal puzzles anymore.

They happened to real communities.

### 2594 — the jurisdiction tribunal

A widely watched dispute forced several governments to recognize that offworld settlements required formal mechanisms for conflict between:
- Earth law;
- settlement charter;
- corporate contract;
- emergency command authority.

The resulting tribunal framework did not grant full autonomy.

It established that frontier residents possessed standing as a political community rather than simply as employees stationed abroad.

This was one of the quiet beginnings of interworld politics.

## Reconstruction rule

The 2587–2594 period is no longer explained by a Homunculus rebellion.

Its central pressures are:
- quarantine;
- route reliability;
- rescue;
- labor;
- settlement rights;
- jurisdiction.

The Homunculus Network remains a separate hidden threat and is not used as a universal historical cause.
