## Opening — the background stops being quiet

The world did not wake up in 2418 knowing disaster was coming.

Nothing opened in the sky.
No city disappeared.
No crystal tower rose from the ground.
No government declared a global emergency.

The change was statistical.

Events that had once been separated by years and continents began appearing often enough to compare.

A hospital recorded an unexplained physiological event.
A remote sensor network measured a field deviation.
A mineral laboratory found a structure that refused to behave as expected.
Wildlife researchers documented a developmental abnormality that did not match ordinary contamination.

Any one case could still be dismissed.

Together, they became harder to ignore.

## The first mistake: assuming one cause too early

By 2420, classified programs possessed enough data to know that the anomaly rate was increasing.

They did not know why.

Several competing theories emerged:
- hidden industrial contamination;
- unknown solar or cosmic forcing;
- a long-period geophysical process;
- deliberate hostile experimentation;
- mutation driven by environmental stress;
- a single undiscovered physical field;
- many unrelated phenomena being grouped together by overconfident analysts.

Each theory explained some data.

None explained all of it.

The most dangerous possibility was also the least politically useful:

that the world itself was entering a new regime.

Governments are better at funding programs that have enemies, locations, budgets, and measurable objectives.

A planetary transition without a known cause was much harder to govern.

## Mineral cases

The first mineral anomalies were disappointing.

That made them credible.

There were no glowing stones powering machines.

Instead, laboratories found tiny structural irregularities:
unexpected lattice persistence,
energy-response behavior outside ordinary material models,
trace inclusions with unusual stability,
samples whose properties changed under conditions that should not matter.

Most samples proved mundane after better analysis.

Some were contamination.
Some were manufacturing artifacts.
Some were bad instruments.

A small number remained unexplained.

SITE_KESSEL_DEEP_MATERIALS_LAB_2426 became important because its researchers did not announce a breakthrough.

They spent eleven years attempting to make the anomaly disappear.

It did not.

## The discipline of failed debunking

The hidden anomaly field had matured enough to distrust excitement.

That culture had been earned through centuries of mistakes.

By the 2430s, a strong case required:
- independent instruments;
- repeated measurement;
- contamination control;
- external replication;
- preserved raw data;
- chain of custody;
- explicit uncertainty;
- review by teams with incentives to disprove the result.

This standard slowed discovery.

It also made the surviving anomalies harder to dismiss.

The field was no longer asking whether strange things happened.

It was asking whether any strange thing survived hostile examination.

More of them did.

## Biology changes first at the margins

Wildlife provided another warning.

Most unusual animals were ordinary:
injured,
diseased,
malnourished,
genetically variant,
exposed to pollution,
or badly identified.

But a few regional monitoring programs began seeing combinations that did not fit known causes.

A mammal population with unexpected tissue mineralization.
A fungal colony that tolerated conditions far outside its normal range.
Microbial communities showing repeated metabolic changes near certain anomaly zones.
Migratory animals avoiding areas that had no visible hazard.

These were not yet Crystal Era beasts.

They did not form stable new food webs.
They did not carry standardized beast crystals.
They did not represent a global ecological transformation.

They were warnings.

## 2441 — Veilwatch

The creation of ORG_VEILWATCH_DIRECTORATE_001 marked a political admission.

The problem could no longer be managed as separate medical, military, mineral, environmental, and intelligence files.

Veilwatch did not replace those institutions.

It connected them.

Its operations center was built around a map room large enough to compare:
- human manifestation reports;
- mineral anomalies;
- spatial events;
- animal and plant irregularities;
- atmospheric and geophysical disturbances;
- classified laboratory measurements.

For the first time, analysts watched multiple anomaly classes on one synchronized operational picture.

That did not reveal the answer.

It revealed the rate of convergence.

## Public life under invisible preparation

The public still did not know.

That meant emergency preparation had to be disguised.

A government could not openly build "anomaly shelters" without explaining anomalies.

So systems were folded into broader resilience programs:
- disaster shelters;
- hazardous-material response;
- grid redundancy;
- hospital surge plans;
- civil-defense drills;
- communications hardening;
- water-system isolation;
- rapid evacuation planning.

These preparations later saved lives during Veinfall.

They were also insufficient because they were designed for regional disasters, not a simultaneous planetary cascade.

## The manifestation rise

Natural human abilities remained rare.

But after 2440, classified reporting began showing a gradual increase in first manifestations.

The increase was not uniform.

Some jurisdictions saw little change.
Others saw statistically significant clusters.

Medical teams debated whether:
- detection had improved;
- reporting had improved;
- social secrecy had weakened;
- environmental conditions were influencing latent compatibility;
- or all of these were true.

The answer was likely mixed.

The important point was that the classified system could no longer assume the historical baseline was stable.

## SITE_VEILWATCH_OPERATIONS_CENTER_2441

The central operations room became famous later because archived wall displays showed how incomplete the picture still was.

Maps contained:
- confirmed events;
- probable events;
- unverified reports;
- instrument anomalies;
- ecological changes;
- false alarms;
- gaps where no trusted data existed.

Analysts resisted the temptation to fill blank areas with guesses.

That restraint preserved credibility.

It also meant decision-makers staring at the map could see a frightening truth:

the world was not fully observed.

## Spatial anomalies

The most politically sensitive cases involved space itself.

Before 2450, most reports of impossible displacement or geometry were rejected.

Some were fraud.
Some were memory errors.
Some were sensor failures.

A handful survived.

Objects appeared centimeters from expected positions.
Laser-ranging paths returned inconsistent values.
Inertial systems registered discontinuities with no corresponding acceleration.
Rooms and tunnels measured correctly one day and produced transient disagreement the next.

Nothing stayed open.

Nothing allowed transit.

No portal existed.

But by 2458, several programs accepted that short-duration spatial distortion was real.

## The Red Basin corridor

SITE_RED_BASIN_SPATIAL_MONITORING_CORRIDOR_2458 became the first place where a spatial anomaly was monitored as a recurring environmental hazard rather than a one-time event.

The corridor was sparsely populated.

That made it easier to instrument and easier to conceal.

Over twelve years, sensors recorded dozens of brief disturbances.

Most lasted less than a second.
A few lasted several seconds.
None formed a stable opening.

The data would later become essential to early portal science.

At the time, it proved only that local space could behave incorrectly.

## 2460s — institutions begin planning for simultaneous failure

Veilwatch war-gamed scenarios that combined:
- grid disruption;
- hospital overload;
- transport failure;
- communications loss;
- hazardous-material release;
- mass panic;
- unexplained biological incidents.

The exercises were useful.

They still assumed that crises would have centers.

A flood has a floodplain.
A fire has a perimeter.
A chemical spill has a source.

Veinfall would violate that assumption.

The future crisis would have thousands of centers.

## The first emergency districts

Several states created multi-hazard response districts around areas with repeated anomalies.

The public explanations were broad:
seismic resilience,
industrial safety,
climate adaptation,
biological security.

Behind the public language, classified planners added anomaly-specific procedures:
- do not approach unknown mineral growth;
- isolate sudden animal behavioral changes;
- record unexplained temperature/field variation;
- avoid entering visibly distorted space;
- separate injured ability users from presumed infectious cases unless evidence supports contagion.

These small procedural details later mattered more than many expensive strategic plans.

## 2471 — too many weak signals

By 2471, Veilwatch analysts faced a problem of abundance.

Human cases were up.
Environmental events were up.
Mineral anomalies were up.
Biological anomalies were up.
Spatial distortions were up.

Yet none had crossed the threshold into the world people would recognize after 2473.

The data looked like pressure without release.

Some analysts predicted a slow decades-long increase.

Others predicted regional disasters.

A small group warned that correlated systems could fail nonlinearly.

No one correctly predicted the eleven-day global cascade.

## 2472 — the threshold year

The final pre-Veinfall year was marked by several changes:

Sealed mineral samples began showing repeatable energy behavior under laboratory conditions.

Spatial distortions in distant regions displayed increasingly similar signatures.

Unusual animal and plant cases clustered near persistent anomaly zones.

Natural ability manifestation reports rose again.

Containment programs that had once dealt with rare cases began competing for staff, laboratory space, transport, and secure storage.

The hidden world was no longer small enough to manage comfortably.

It was still hidden from the public.

That contradiction could not last.

## Ordinary people in 2472

Most people went to work.

Children attended school.
Markets opened.
Families argued.
People traveled.
Hospitals treated ordinary disease.
Governments debated ordinary policy.

The world did not feel like it was ending.

That matters.

Veinfall was catastrophic not because humanity had been living in constant visible supernatural chaos.

It was catastrophic because ordinary civilization still functioned.

People had routines to lose.

Homes to evacuate.
Jobs to abandon.
Infrastructure to fail.
Pets and livestock to protect.
Medicine to refrigerate.
Children to find.
Relatives to call.

The historical weight of Veinfall comes from the normality immediately before it.

- biological irregularities cluster near some anomaly zones;
