# BOOK 06 — Epilogue
## The World Has Teeth

Status: ACTIVE_HISTORY_BOOK_DRAFT_CANON
Temporal anchor: 2605 reference perspective
Purpose: lived bridge from Portal Expansion into creature/ecology reference
Live-game clock effect: NONE

By 2605, the dangerous thing about a beast was no longer simply that it could kill someone.

The dangerous thing was that it belonged somewhere.

A pack predator near a road implied prey nearby.
A starving pack implied something had changed farther up the food chain.
A burrow under a utility corridor was an engineering problem before it was a hunting problem.
A dead offworld animal could become a quarantine event.
A migrating herd could close a settlement road without attacking anyone.

The frontier learned to ask a different question.

Not:

"How strong is it?"

But:

"Why is it here?"

### A responder's map

An experienced field team looked at more than tracks.

They looked at:
- water;
- shelter;
- prey;
- breeding season;
- wind;
- terrain;
- recent construction;
- abandoned waste;
- portal freight;
- carcasses;
- local reports.

A creature encounter was evidence about the landscape.

### Strength was a bad universal scale

A Stonecrown Grazer could be physically stronger than a Glassfang Stalker and still be less dangerous in most circumstances.

A Mirecoil could be manageable in open water but dangerous in a narrow dock channel.

A small scavenger carrying an unfamiliar parasite could create consequences larger than a predator attack.

Operational threat therefore became contextual.

The classification system that emerged did not ask only what the organism could do.

It asked:
- how many;
- where;
- against whom;
- under what conditions;
- with what ecological consequence.

### Hunters changed too

Professional harvesting became more disciplined because a valuable animal was often valuable alive as a population.

Removing a breeding adult could destroy future supply.

Damaging a crystal during a kill could erase much of its value.

Killing a predator could cause herbivores to explode in number.

Field teams therefore learned that precision was ecological as well as tactical.

### The Academy inheritance

Later Academy training would inherit this principle:

A creature is not a combat encounter first.

It is an organism first.

That distinction is one reason trained responders inspect terrain, carcasses, population signs, disease, and local law before deciding what force is justified.

## Author-use rule

Future scenes should treat beasts as parts of living systems.

A creature appears because:
- its range supports it;
- its food supports it;
- its history supports it;
- a route brought it;
- displacement moved it;
- human activity attracted it;
- or a specific exceptional event explains the violation.

If none of those are true, the creature does not belong in the scene.
