# BOOK 03 — Veinfall
## Chapter 7 — Eighteen Months of Not Normal

Status: ACTIVE_HISTORY_BOOK_DRAFT_CANON

Veinfall's first eleven days ended.

The emergency did not.

For roughly eighteen months, humanity lived in a world where the difference between temporary disruption and permanent change was not yet clear.

Every week produced the same question in a different form:

Was this still Veinfall, or was this simply life now?

### The first month

In many regions, the earliest priority was not research.

It was shelter.

People had evacuated from:
- crystallization belts;
- recurring breach corridors;
- damaged urban districts;
- contaminated watersheds;
- unstable transport routes;
- biologically altered rural zones.

Emergency shelters filled beyond design capacity.

Schools became dormitories.

Warehouses became ration depots.

Sports halls became triage centers.

Parking structures became controlled vehicle and decontamination zones.

Unused office buildings became temporary family housing.

The physical form of the city changed faster than any planning law could keep up.

### From shelter to district

A shelter is designed around departure.

A refugee district is designed around the possibility that departure may not happen soon.

That difference changed everything.

By the second and third months, large emergency sites began acquiring:
- assigned sleeping sectors;
- sanitation blocks;
- cooking areas;
- water distribution points;
- medical isolation;
- child-care spaces;
- security posts;
- registration desks;
- schooling;
- repair workshops;
- ration queues;
- informal markets.

Families marked boundaries with blankets, tape, salvaged panels, storage crates, and furniture.

People created addresses inside places that had never been neighborhoods.

A numbered cot became a row.

A row became a section.

A section became a district.

### Food became a geography problem

The world still had food.

The problem was moving safe food through unsafe geography.

Some agricultural regions remained productive but lost access to transport.

Some transport corridors remained open but crossed contaminated zones.

Some farms looked healthy until soil tests failed.

Some livestock survived but could not be moved through quarantine.

Some ports operated under strict biological inspection that reduced throughput dramatically.

The first ration systems were therefore logistical, not simply scarcity-based.

A city could have grain within two hundred kilometers and still face rationing if:
- the rail line crossed an exclusion zone;
- the bridge was closed;
- the depot lacked decontamination capacity;
- transport workers refused the route;
- authorities could not certify the crop.

### Water was worse

Food could be rerouted.

Water could not always be.

A contaminated river, reservoir, intake pipe, or groundwater field could disable an entire district even if every building remained intact.

Water plants began installing emergency isolation loops.

Mobile purification units became strategic equipment.

Communities learned new terms:
- upstream exclusion;
- intake quarantine;
- sediment anomaly;
- crystal particulate;
- biological unknown;
- boil advisory;
- field contamination.

In some districts, residents could see clean-looking water from their windows and were forbidden to drink it.

### The first black markets

Crystals had no mature industrial value yet.

That did not stop people from assigning value to them.

Rumors spread that certain samples:
- healed wounds;
- improved strength;
- powered devices;
- predicted portal activity;
- increased manifestation probability;
- protected against exposure.

Most claims were wrong, exaggerated, or dangerously incomplete.

But raw crystal material was rare, visible, and associated with extraordinary events.

That made it tradable.

Early smugglers entered restricted zones to recover:
- crystal fragments;
- altered tissue;
- animal remains;
- breach debris;
- abandoned laboratory material.

Some sold to collectors.

Some sold to private laboratories.

Some sold to criminal groups.

Some sold to desperate families seeking miracle treatments.

The first crystal black market grew before the legal crystal market existed.

### Creature-control before the word had a profession

Police were not trained to track altered predators.

Animal-control units were not equipped for crystal-bearing anatomy.

Military units could kill dangerous animals, but not every altered animal was an enemy and indiscriminate killing could make ecological problems worse.

The first specialized response teams emerged from combinations of:
- wildlife officers;
- veterinarians;
- hazardous-material technicians;
- military marksmen;
- field biologists;
- emergency medics.

Their first doctrine was simple:

Identify.
Isolate.
Observe if possible.
Kill only when necessary.
Recover safely.
Document everything.

This doctrine would become more sophisticated later.

At the time, it was revolutionary because it rejected the assumption that every altered organism should simply be destroyed.

### Natural ability or exposure injury?

Hospitals faced a similarly difficult distinction.

Two patients might both produce heat.

One might possess a natural ability that had manifested under stress.

The other might have unstable crystal contamination causing uncontrolled thermal discharge.

Treating them as the same condition could kill one of them.

By the middle of the extended-instability period, specialist hospitals began using provisional criteria:
- prior family history;
- pre-Veinfall anomaly records;
- crystal burden in tissue;
- persistence outside anomaly zones;
- metabolic pattern;
- neurological signature;
- reproducibility under controlled conditions.

The result was not certainty.

It was better uncertainty.

### Borders inside countries

Exclusion zones created internal borders.

Checkpoints separated:
- safe from unsafe;
- certified from uncertified cargo;
- residents from evacuees;
- clean vehicles from contaminated vehicles;
- permitted workers from the general public.

Some exclusion zones shrank.

Some expanded.

Some became permanent.

Communities cut off by those boundaries developed distinct economies and identities.

A town five kilometers from a persistent zone could live under different rules than a town fifty kilometers away.

### The eighteen-month psychological shift

At first, people waited for restoration.

Then they waited for stabilization.

Then many stopped waiting.

New jobs appeared.

Children began school inside temporary districts.

Businesses reopened around checkpoints.

Families relocated permanently.

Governments created offices that had no pre-Veinfall equivalent.

Researchers received permanent budgets.

Military units created standing anomaly-response commands.

Insurance contracts were rewritten.

Building codes began changing.

By the end of the extended-instability period, civilization had not solved the Crystal Era.

It had accepted that the Crystal Era was not going away.

## What changed by chapter end

PUBLIC:
- exclusion zones, altered organisms, ability users, and recurring breaches are understood as enduring parts of life;
- rationing and relocation become normalized in heavily affected regions;
- early black markets are widely known.

CLASSIFIED:
- governments maintain prioritized lists of persistent crystal and breach sites;
- specialist medical and biological datasets begin separating natural manifestation from exposure pathology;
- recurring biological and portal corridors are mapped.

TRUE:
- ecological succession has begun in persistent zones;
- some altered lineages are reproducing successfully;
- some offworld organisms have established small populations;
- institutional responses created during emergency are becoming permanent.
