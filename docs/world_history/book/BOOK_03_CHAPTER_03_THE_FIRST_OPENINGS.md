The first breaches were not doorways.

They were failures of separation.

### Before anyone crossed

The earliest persistent-enough breaches were detected because the environment around them stopped behaving locally.

Air pressure shifted without a weather system.

Sound propagated through empty space with delays that instruments could not reconcile.

Condensation formed on one side of a boundary while the surrounding air remained dry.

Objects near the boundary experienced force without visible contact.

The safest early response was distance.

That lesson was learned unevenly.

### Pressure killed before creatures did

One of the first recurring public misconceptions was that the danger began when organisms came through.

At several breach sites, the first casualties resulted from atmosphere and pressure.

A connection to another environment does not need a predator to be lethal. A pressure differential can turn loose debris into projectiles. Toxic gases can spread faster than responders can identify them. Water crossing through a low boundary can flood enclosed terrain. Extreme temperature can injure from the edge of a site.

Emergency doctrine began with improvised exclusion circles because nobody yet knew how to shape the boundary.

The radius was often set by the farthest observed effect plus whatever distance local authorities could physically enforce.

### What crossed first

Matter transfer did not wait for exploration teams.

Dust crossed.

Spores crossed.

Microorganisms crossed.

Water crossed at some sites.

Fragments of plants and small organisms crossed through others.

Only after repeated monitoring did responders begin distinguishing one-time contamination from organisms capable of surviving Earth conditions.

This is why the ecological history of portals begins before the history of portal travel.

### The first animals

The first confirmed offworld animals were not giant apex predators.

Small organisms were more likely to cross unnoticed and survive temporary exposure.

Some died within minutes.

Some survived hours.

A few found food, shelter, temperature, and chemistry close enough to their requirements.

At the same time, terrestrial animals near breach zones were already being injured or altered by crystal-associated exposure.

For frightened civilians and exhausted responders, origin was irrelevant. Anything unfamiliar near a breach could be called a monster.

For later science, the distinction became essential:
- altered Earth lineage;
- true offworld lineage;
- transient exposure victim;
- stable crystal-adapted organism.

### Quarantine geometry

The first quarantine perimeters were physical geography made into policy.

Roads became boundaries because barriers and personnel already existed there.

Rivers were used where crossing points could be controlled.

Industrial fences became emergency containment lines.

In cities, a single block might be closed while the next remained occupied because authorities lacked space to evacuate farther.

At remote sites, exclusion radii could extend kilometers.

That inequality shaped early casualty patterns. Dense settlements could not create the safety distances that remote research ranges could.

### Day 5

By the end of Day 5, humanity had not learned to travel through portals.

It had learned something more basic and more frightening:

A breach could connect Earth to an environment that did not care whether Earth was ready.

The problem was no longer only what Veinfall was doing to Earth.

Earth was now exchanging matter with somewhere else.

## What changed by chapter end

PUBLIC:
- spatial breaches are confirmed;
- evacuation and quarantine become common crisis vocabulary;
- rumors of "other worlds" spread faster than scientific confirmation.

CLASSIFIED:
- some breach signatures recur;
- destinations appear to have environmental consistency;
- controlled probes remain unreliable and dangerous.

TRUE:
- multiple breaches connect to genuinely distinct environments;
- cross-ecology transfer has begun;
- later invasion/ecological problems already have microscopic beginnings.

## Unresolved

- whether any route can be reopened deliberately;
- whether return transit is possible;
- whether intelligent nonhuman civilizations exist;
- which transferred organisms can establish reproducing populations.
