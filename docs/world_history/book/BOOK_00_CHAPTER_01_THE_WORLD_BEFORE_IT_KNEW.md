## Opening

Long before anyone used the word *crystal* as a historical dividing line, Earth was simply Earth.

There were no licensed beast hunters, no portal sirens, no field-stability maps, no school drills explaining what to do if an aperture opened inside a building. There were no crystal exchanges, no quarantine corridors between worlds, no ability registration offices, and no academies built around the assumption that a teenager might one day have to fight something that did not evolve on Earth.

The planet had no reason to expect any of those things.

Its mountains rose because plates collided. Oceans deepened and narrowed. Species appeared, spread, competed, and disappeared. Forests changed with climate. Predators followed prey. Parasites learned new hosts. Catastrophes came from geology, atmosphere, disease, impact, famine, and ordinary biological competition.

Later generations would look backward and search this ancient world for signs that the Crystal Era had always been waiting underneath it. Some found unusual mineral ratios or isotopic patterns that seemed too old to fit later models. Others argued those findings were contamination, bad assumptions, or geological processes misunderstood through a post-Veinfall lens.

No accepted evidence proves that prehistoric Earth was secretly a crystal world.

That uncertainty matters.

If every strange fossil, every mass extinction, every ancient mutation, and every unexplained mineral seam were eventually blamed on crystals, then the old Earth would stop being a real world and become nothing more than a prologue designed to predict the future. The historical record does not support that.

Earth lived for billions of years without humanity and, as far as current canon can prove, without a stable crystal biosphere.

## A planet that did not need the extraordinary

The deep history of Earth is not empty background. It establishes the baseline against which every later anomaly has meaning.

Life survived by ordinary mechanisms.

Plants and photosynthetic organisms converted light into chemical energy. Microbes colonized places that would kill larger organisms. Predators evolved to catch prey; prey evolved to avoid them. Disease shaped populations. Climate pushed species into new regions or erased them from old ones. Reproduction, migration, mutation, selection, symbiosis, and extinction built ecosystems that could persist for thousands or millions of years without any crystal-derived energy source.

There was no universal category called a *beast*.

A dangerous predator was still an animal.

An organism did not contain a harvestable crystal core simply because it was large, venomous, armored, or difficult to kill. No stable crystal-energy trophic layer sat above ordinary food webs. Nothing in the surviving record suggests that prehistoric ecology depended on the phenomena that would later transform biological life after Veinfall.

This old ecological normality would eventually become scientifically precious.

Centuries after Veinfall, ecologists trying to decide whether an organism was native, altered, offworld, engineered, or fully crystalized would repeatedly return to pre-Crystal genetic archives and fossil records. Without that baseline, there would be no reliable way to tell what Earth had become.

## The emergence of humanity

Homo sapiens did not begin as a separate supernatural species.

Humans emerged through terrestrial evolution and carried all the limitations that implied: bodies that needed food and water, brains shaped by social learning, bones that broke, infections that killed, children who required long care, and communities that survived by memory, cooperation, competition, and adaptation.

The first humans did not enter a world already arranged around them.

They moved through landscapes occupied by other predators. They learned seasonal cycles. They followed water and game. They made tools, controlled fire, buried the dead, told stories, and remembered places where danger waited.

What later researchers would call the anomalous human coupling phenotype existed somewhere inside this species' biological possibility, but it did not define humanity.

There is no confirmed prehuman hominin record showing a stable manifested ability. That does not prove such manifestations never occurred. It means the surviving evidence is not strong enough to convert possibility into canon.

By the time anatomically modern humans were established across expanding regions, however, the first evidence of something unusual becomes harder to dismiss.

Not obvious.

Not common.

Not understood.

But present.

## The first difficult evidence

Modern reconstruction places the earliest credible ancestral evidence for anomalous coupling somewhere around 70,000–50,000 BCE.

No name survives.

No verified story survives.

No one can say with confidence whether the individual or individuals involved could lift impossible weight, perceive something others could not, heal abnormally, influence matter, or manifest any effect that would later fit a modern ability category.

What survives is indirect.

Paleogenetic reconstruction, population comparison, and later compatibility models suggest that at least one early human population carried an ancestral form of the rare biological architecture associated with naturally manifested abilities.

That does not mean one gifted ancestor created every ability user who came afterward.

The trait was not a single simple gene and did not pass like a predictable eye color. It was a complex predisposition. Some descendants could carry compatibility without ever manifesting anything unusual. Others could manifest very different effects. Many lineages vanished.

For the people living at the time, none of that scientific language existed.

An unusual child was a child.

A person who survived something impossible was a story.

A hunter who sensed danger before anyone else might be admired, feared, ignored, or accused of exaggeration.

A healer whose patients survived unusually often might become important to a community without anyone understanding why.

And because manifestations were extraordinarily rare, no village, tribe, or migrating population had enough cases to build a global theory.

The phenomenon could remain real without becoming recognized.

## Why the secret could survive without a conspiracy

One of the easiest mistakes later historians made was assuming that if abilities existed before Veinfall, then someone must have controlled the secret.

There is no evidence for a single ancient organization with that kind of reach.

The reality was messier.

Population density was low. Travel was slow. Literacy did not exist for most of the period. Oral traditions changed. Communities disappeared. Languages split. Records, where they eventually existed, could burn, rot, be conquered, translated badly, or be interpreted through religious and cultural assumptions.

Most importantly, the numbers worked against recognition.

Even in much later pre-Veinfall populations, a broad historical estimate places active manifested users at roughly one person per five to twenty million humans, with higher local concentrations in a few unusual lineages.

In early human history, when total population itself was far smaller, generations could pass without a community encountering an obvious manifestation.

A real phenomenon can remain invisible when it is rare enough.

## The human world before history

As humanity spread, the ordinary problems of survival mattered far more than anomalies.

Food had to be found.

Children had to survive infancy.

Injury had to be treated with whatever knowledge was available.

Weather could destroy a season.

A river changing course could erase a settlement.

Predators competed with humans for territory.

Conflict between human groups could be as dangerous as anything in the wilderness.

The first anomalous humans therefore did not grow up in a civilization prepared to train them.

They grew up inside whatever family, community, belief system, or crisis happened to surround them.

An unusual ability could be useful.

It could also be socially catastrophic.

A manifestation that helped hunting might be accepted.

One associated with seizures, uncontrolled heat, altered appearance, voices, visions, or unexplained injury could be interpreted as sickness, curse, possession, divine favor, contamination, deception, or threat.

There was no universal answer because there was no universal human culture.

## The first inheritance problem

As some human groups became more stable and populations grew, recurrent patterns began to appear.

A grandmother who had always known when storms were coming.

A grandson whose senses seemed impossible.

A family in which wounds closed faster than expected.

Another family that produced nothing unusual for several generations and then, without warning, a child whose body behaved differently from everyone else's.

Later science would interpret these patterns as probabilistic inheritance.

The old world interpreted them through memory.

Families remembered.

Clans built taboos.

Communities created stories.

Some people concealed difference because difference was dangerous.

Others built identity around it.

The important shift came much later, with permanent settlement.

Once humans stopped moving constantly and began building larger communities, family history became easier to preserve. Property, status, kinship, ritual, marriage, and inheritance made bloodlines socially visible in ways that mobile populations had not sustained as easily.

That created the conditions for persistent anomalous lineages.

It did not yet create a supernatural society.

## The world at the edge of recorded history

By roughly 12,000–6,000 BCE, the transition toward agriculture and permanent settlement in multiple regions changed what humanity could remember.

A rare manifestation no longer had to disappear completely when one person died.

It could become part of family reputation.

It could affect marriage.

It could influence who was trusted with healing, hunting, ritual, leadership, or warfare.

It could also attract fear.

This is where the next chapter begins.

Not with a secret empire.

Not with crystals falling from the sky.

Not with a hidden species waiting to conquer humanity.

It begins with people.

Families.

Stories.

And a phenomenon so rare that humanity could live beside it for thousands of years without understanding what it was.

## Knowledge layers

### PUBLIC historical understanding in 2670

Mainstream education teaches:
- Earth and life developed through ordinary natural processes;
- Homo sapiens is a terrestrial species;
- Veinfall marks the start of the Crystal Era, not the origin of life;
- natural ability users existed before Veinfall, though early evidence is sparse.

### CLASSIFIED / specialist understanding

Specialist archives contain:
- disputed deep-time mineral anomalies;
- paleogenetic evidence suggesting ancient anomalous compatibility;
- lineage models extending farther back than public textbooks usually emphasize.

### TRUE author-history

- ordinary terrestrial evolution is real and remains the baseline;
- natural anomalous potential appears inside Homo sapiens rather than defining a separate species;
- the first exact manifested abilities remain unrecoverable;
- the ultimate reason humans can couple to anomalous phenomena remains UNKNOWN.

## End condition

By the end of this chapter, humanity possesses:
- expanding populations;
- permanent settlements;
- stronger lineage memory;
- the biological possibility of rare natural abilities;
- no unified theory of those abilities;
- no public crystal civilization;
- no stable beast ecology;
- no portal infrastructure.

The stage is set for the first long age of hidden anomalous humanity.
