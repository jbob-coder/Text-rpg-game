By 2505, a city could look normal from a distance.

Traffic moved.
Schools opened.
Markets operated.
Hospitals treated ordinary illness.
Families went to work.

The difference appeared in the systems underneath.

A hospital had isolation architecture that did not exist before Veinfall.

A freight terminal had crystal inspection bays.

A school had manifestation protocols.

A wildlife map could carry strategic importance.

A military unit might train for a breach that had not opened in years.

The Early Crystal Era ended not when danger disappeared, but when society learned to design around it.

### Standing forces replaced emergency detachments

Military doctrine changed slowly.

Veinfall-era units had been assembled for immediate crisis response.

By the late 2490s, permanent formations appeared around recurring needs:
- exclusion-zone security;
- dangerous fauna;
- breach perimeter defense;
- hazardous material transport;
- ability-user tactical support;
- evacuation.

These units needed standardized training, logistics, and facilities.

Their equipment was still mostly conventional.

Their doctrine was no longer conventional.

Commanders learned that force had to integrate:
- biologists;
- medical personnel;
- engineers;
- intelligence;
- tactical units;
- logistics.

A rifle could stop some threats.

It could not identify a pathogen, stabilize a breach, or tell whether killing a territorial predator would send a herd into a populated district.

### The Academy idea begins to take shape

The term "Academy" was not yet one universal institution.

But by the turn of the century, governments recognized a structural problem.

Separate schools produced specialists who understood their own field but not the others.

Creature responders lacked portal doctrine.
Engineers lacked ability-user de-escalation.
Military cadets lacked ecological training.
Medical personnel lacked field-security experience.

Several jurisdictions began building joint-service anomaly colleges.

Their purpose was not to create super-soldiers.

Their purpose was to train people who could operate where biology, crystals, abilities, law, and security overlapped.

These campuses are direct institutional ancestors of later military-academy systems.

They remain historically distinct from the modern Academy of the game era.

### Civilian life in 2505

A resident of a major city in 2505 might encounter Crystal Era systems without ever entering an exclusion zone.

At a hospital:
a specialized scan might use a supervised crystal-assisted sensor.

At a freight terminal:
sealed cargo might pass through anomaly inspection.

At school:
students might practice a manifestation emergency drill once a term.

At work:
certain jobs required exposure screening.

At the edge of town:
a road sign could warn that an ecological buffer was legally protected.

At home:
most people still used conventional electricity, conventional appliances, conventional transport, and conventional communications.

Crystal technology had entered civilization.

It had not replaced civilization.

### Insurance became an invisible regulator

Governments wrote law.

Insurers often decided what actually got built.

A laboratory without adequate setback could not obtain coverage.

A hospital using a crystal-assisted system needed:
- inspection;
- containment;
- trained operators;
- shutdown procedure;
- incident reporting.

A warehouse holding regulated material required structural separation.

Insurance standards pushed architecture toward repeatable safety before international law fully harmonized.

This pressure helped prepare the ground for the 2506 standards conference.

### The last problem of the Early Crystal Era

By 2505, the world had many working regional systems.

That was precisely the problem.

A sample certified in one region could be rejected in another.

A transport container legal at one border might require repacking at the next.

Laboratory measurements could still differ enough to complicate trade.

Training credentials were not universally recognized.

Military terminology varied.

The world had learned how to use crystals.

It had not yet learned how to agree on them.

That conflict defines the transition into Crystal Industrialization.

## End of Early Crystal Era

By 2505:
- limited civilian crystal applications are real;
- regional legal markets exist;
- insurance and certification are mature enough to constrain design;
- standing anomaly-response forces exist;
- protected beast habitats and controlled harvest policy exist;
- ability jurisprudence has durable principles but remains jurisdictional;
- integrated anomaly-training colleges are emerging;
- portal science remains observational/stabilization-oriented;
- artificial portal travel does not exist;
- global crystal standards do not yet exist.

The next year changes that trajectory.

2506 begins the standardization phase that makes industrial scaling possible.
