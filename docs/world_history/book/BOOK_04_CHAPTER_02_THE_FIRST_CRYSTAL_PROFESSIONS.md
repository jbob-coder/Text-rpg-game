The second stage of the Early Crystal Era began when survival work became ordinary employment.

By 2478, a teenager could imagine becoming a crystal-storage technician, anomaly medic, field ecologist, quarantine inspector, or creature-response specialist as a career rather than an emergency assignment.

That change was more important than any single laboratory discovery.

A civilization had begun to reproduce the knowledge needed to survive its new world.

### Standardization changed trust

Early crystal science had a problem: two laboratories could describe the same sample in incompatible language.

One called a specimen stable.

Another called it dormant.

A third called it biologically inactive.

A transport authority only wanted to know whether the container could go through a city.

The first serious interlaboratory standards therefore focused less on explaining crystals and more on making measurements comparable.

Researchers began recording:
- source;
- mass;
- visible structure;
- purity estimate;
- thermal behavior;
- field response;
- biological activity;
- contamination;
- storage requirement.

A label became a form of infrastructure.

### The new field worker

Creature-response work became a profession with a recognizable kit.

A typical trained team did not carry only weapons.

It carried:
- sealed sample containers;
- respirators;
- protective suits;
- field analyzers;
- tagging equipment;
- veterinary sedatives;
- trauma supplies;
- restraint tools;
- ranged weapons for lethal emergency use;
- portable barriers;
- decontamination packs;
- mapping beacons.

The goal was not simply to kill the animal.

The goal was to understand what had happened, prevent secondary damage, and preserve information.

### Occupational medicine

Workers in crystal labs, exclusion zones, quarantine yards, altered ecosystems, and creature-response units began developing recurring health problems.

Not all were exotic.

Heat stress.

Respiratory disease.

Chemical exposure.

Sleep disruption.

Chronic fear.

Musculoskeletal injuries.

But some were new:
- crystal particulate retention;
- field-associated neurological symptoms;
- tissue mineralization;
- intermittent sensory distortion;
- repeated low-level biological exposure;
- stress-linked involuntary ability activation.

Occupational medicine expanded because the Crystal Era had created jobs that the human body had not evolved to perform safely.

### Three rebuilt settlements

Recovery did not produce one urban form.

In the Carolina Resilience District, the refugee grid became a permanent mixed residential/service municipality. Its straight modular streets preserved the logic of emergency logistics.

At Greenvale, rebuilding spread around biological buffers rather than through them. The town acquired an irregular edge shaped by wildlife corridors, quarantine land, and protected drainage.

At East Harbor, vertical density remained necessary. New buildings were designed around isolation floors, roof evacuation, protected utility shafts, and rapid compartment shutdown.

Architecture became local memory.

Each settlement embodied the disaster it had survived.

### The first generation trained after Veinfall

By the mid-2480s, students were entering specialist schools who had been children during Veinfall or had been born after it.

Their instructors often carried direct trauma from the crisis.

That created a culture of caution.

Old responders taught:
- never trust one sensor;
- never enter a zone without a return route;
- never assume an animal is alone;
- never assume "inert" means safe;
- never confuse an ability user with an exposure victim without evidence.

These principles later became doctrine.

At the time, they were stories instructors repeated because someone they knew had died learning them.

### The social divide

Crystal-era professions paid well when they were dangerous and scarce.

That created new social mobility for some people.

It also created exploitation.

Poorer districts supplied workers for:
- cleanup;
- hazardous transport;
- carcass recovery;
- exclusion-zone construction;
- sample processing.

Wealthier institutions controlled:
- patents;
- laboratories;
- licenses;
- insurance;
- secure storage;
- high-value research.

The Crystal Era created opportunities.

It also created new classes of risk.

### By 2488

Humanity still could not manufacture reliable portals.

It did not possess a mature crystal economy.

It did not fully understand beast evolution.

But it had achieved something equally important:

repeatability.

A trained worker could follow a protocol written by someone they had never met.

A laboratory could compare results with another continent.

A city could design a quarantine facility based on prior failures.

A school could graduate people specifically trained for the Crystal Era.

The emergency had become knowledge.

Knowledge had become institutions.

Institutions were about to become industry.
