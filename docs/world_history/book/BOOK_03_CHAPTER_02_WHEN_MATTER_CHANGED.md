The second phase of Veinfall began before humanity had finished naming the first.

During the first six hours, instruments had contradicted assumptions. During the next twenty-four, physical samples began contradicting the categories themselves.

### Crystals did not arrive in one way

Later popular history often reduced the event to crystal deposits appearing across Earth. The first day was less orderly.

In some rock formations, mineral lattices reorganized along fractures already present in the stone.

In some water systems, suspended particles assembled into short-lived structures and then dissolved.

Inside industrial materials, narrow filaments formed at stress boundaries and destroyed parts that had passed inspection hours earlier.

In biological tissue, the overwhelming outcome of uncontrolled crystallization was injury or death, not enhancement.

Some formations produced measurable energy interactions.

Some were inert after formation.

Some disintegrated when removed.

Some became more stable inside living tissue than outside it.

The word "crystal" therefore described appearance before it described science.

### The Kessel vault

Kessel became one of the first places where experts understood that contamination was not an adequate explanation.

Its sample vault was only thirteen meters long and nine meters wide. That small space mattered historically because it contained pre-Veinfall specimens collected under different programs and stored separately.

Several changed within the same hours despite differing origin and containment.

The physical separation that should have disproved a common mechanism instead strengthened the case for one.

Researchers moved the most reactive specimens into isolation alcoves. Two alcoves suffered internal structural damage when crystal growth followed metal joints rather than the sample boundaries technicians expected.

Nobody at Kessel possessed a later-era hazard taxonomy. Workers improvised distance, shielding, remote handling, and evacuation rules while the event was underway.

### Infrastructure becomes material risk

The crisis widened because modern civilization depended on material reliability.

A bridge does not have to collapse to become unusable; inspectors only need to lose confidence that its load assumptions remain valid.

A hospital does not need to lose all electricity; it becomes unsafe when backup systems behave differently from their certification.

A water plant does not need visible poison; it must shut intake when its sensors detect unknown particulate growth that standard treatment cannot classify.

Across the world, authorities closed infrastructure faster than physical destruction alone would have required because uncertainty became its own hazard.

That decision saved lives in some regions and stranded populations in others.

### The first recovery markets

Even before the material was understood, people began collecting it.

Some did so for science.

Some because governments paid for samples.

Some because rumors claimed the new material could heal, power devices, increase abilities, or be sold for enormous sums.

Within eighteen hours, emergency authorities in several regions were already confiscating unlicensed samples after burns, poisoning, structural failures, and unexplained biological reactions.

The first crystal black markets began before a legitimate crystal economy existed.

### The biological dividing line

Animals and plants exposed to the new conditions did not follow one path.

Many died.

Some showed acute tissue failure.

Some survived with temporary changes that disappeared.

A much smaller number stabilized altered tissues.

That distinction—between exposure, survival, and heritable adaptation—would take decades to understand. On the first day, emergency responders had no way to know whether an animal with crystalline growths was dying, becoming dangerous, or becoming the ancestor of a stable new lineage.

The later category "beast" did not yet exist as a scientific classification.

### V+30:00

At thirty hours, Veinfall had already changed economics, medicine, infrastructure, and emergency law without a single government understanding its full mechanism.

The world still had no controlled portal.

It had no mature crystal industry.

It had no standardized beast ranks.

What it had was a growing number of places where ordinary assumptions about matter could no longer be trusted.

Then space began to remain open.
