## Core distinction

A portal is not simply an always-open doorway.

Operational portal systems contain several distinct components:
- aperture generation;
- destination addressing;
- stabilization;
- power/control;
- transit envelope;
- return-path management;
- quarantine;
- security.

Failure of any layer can make transit impossible or lethal.

## Addressing

Portal destinations are referenced through measured resonance signatures rather than ordinary geographic coordinates alone.

A destination signature may drift because of:
- local field change;
- geological change;
- orbital/planetary motion;
- interference;
- nearby portal activity;
- unknown anomalous processes.

Therefore portal routes require periodic recalibration.

## Route classes

### ROUTE-P0 — Natural recurring breach
Observed, not created.

### ROUTE-P1 — Anchored natural route
Natural breach artificially stabilized.

### ROUTE-P2 — Artificial experimental route
Created by human equipment but not sufficiently reliable for routine personnel transport.

### ROUTE-P3 — Certified controlled route
Repeatable destination and return under regulated conditions.

### ROUTE-P4 — Strategic corridor
High-capacity, heavily defended route supporting settlements, military logistics, or major trade.

### ROUTE-PX — Unbounded/unknown
Route behavior cannot be safely modeled.

## Hub architecture

A mature portal hub includes:
- anchor chamber;
- redundant power;
- emergency closure;
- atmospheric isolation;
- decontamination;
- medical triage;
- customs/security;
- cargo staging;
- destination monitoring;
- quarantine holding;
- armed response;
- black-box event recording.

## Failure modes

- destination mismatch;
- partial aperture;
- pressure shock;
- field collapse during transit;
- return-route loss;
- biological contamination;
- hostile incursion;
- overload;
- sabotage;
- signature spoofing;
- cargo instability.

## Worldbuilding rule

Travel time through the aperture may be negligible, but total journey time is not.

Real travel includes:
- permits;
- queues;
- cargo inspection;
- quarantine;
- calibration;
- weather/environment checks;
- security;
- possible route closure.

This prevents portal travel from erasing geography, borders, logistics, or scarcity.
