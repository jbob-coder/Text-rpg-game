# THE GAME — Primary Ability Rarity Standard

Status: **ACTIVE TARGET-GAME DESIGN / CANONICAL CLASSIFICATION STANDARD**

Parent:
- `../STATUS_UI_ABILITIES_AND_PASSIVES_MASTER_PLAN.md`
- `STATUS_UI_CORE_CONTRACT.md`

Purpose: give the human primary-ability rarity ladder stable meaning without pretending every ability of the same rarity has identical combat strength.

---

# 1. Canon rarity order

The canonical order is:

1. **Common**
2. **Uncommon**
3. **Rare**
4. **Super Rare**
5. **Epic**
6. **Super Epic**
7. **Legendary**
8. **Prime Legendary**
9. **Unique**

This order is fixed unless the owner explicitly changes it.

---

# 2. What rarity means

Rarity is primarily a classification of **potential, scarcity, breadth/depth of possible development, and the exceptional nature of the awakened ability**.

Rarity is not a guaranteed outcome.

A higher-rarity ability may have:
- greater raw ceiling;
- stronger initial manifestation;
- broader technique space;
- more extreme evolution potential;
- rarer governing law;
- stronger interaction with stats/resources;
- unusual rule-breaking behavior;
- fewer known counters;
- larger strategic value;
- stronger institutional interest.

A lower-rarity user can still defeat a higher-rarity user through:
- superior Level;
- better stats;
- skill;
- experience;
- passives;
- tactical preparation;
- knowledge;
- favorable matchup;
- equipment;
- environment;
- teamwork;
- exploiting drawbacks.

The setting must therefore acknowledge real inequality between abilities without reducing combat to rarity-versus-rarity.

---

# 3. Classification dimensions

Every primary ability receives separate values for:

- rarity tier;
- occurrence frequency;
- raw output ceiling;
- utility breadth;
- technique depth;
- evolution potential;
- difficulty of mastery;
- resource burden;
- drawback severity;
- counter availability;
- social/institutional value;
- secrecy/classification status.

Do not collapse these into one hidden “power score.”

---

# 4. Common

## Design identity

Common abilities are the most frequently observed and best understood.

Typical properties:
- narrow or practical domain;
- relatively predictable manifestation;
- widely documented training methods;
- many known users historically;
- established counters;
- common institutional policies.

Common does **not** mean useless.

A Common ability may become extremely dangerous when paired with:
- very high Level;
- elite training;
- strong passives;
- advanced technique;
- intelligent application.

Common abilities provide a critical baseline for civilian life, professions, security, industry, survival, and ordinary combat.

---

# 5. Uncommon

Uncommon abilities are noticeably less frequent and usually offer a more distinctive application, stronger baseline, or more unusual mechanic than Common abilities.

Typical properties:
- less standardized training;
- fewer expert mentors;
- greater variation between users;
- more institutional attention;
- wider or more specialized technique potential.

---

# 6. Rare

Rare abilities represent a meaningful jump in scarcity and potential.

Typical properties:
- unusual governing principle or high-value application;
- limited historical user pool;
- significant recruitment/monitoring interest;
- fewer complete training records;
- stronger strategic impact.

A Rare awakening can materially affect a person's education, employment, social treatment, and faction interest.

---

# 7. Super Rare

Super Rare abilities are exceptional enough that individual awakenings can become notable beyond a local institution.

Typical properties:
- very small known user population;
- powerful or unusually flexible mechanics;
- incomplete counter doctrine;
- highly valuable specialist applications;
- elevated government/military/research attention.

Public handling may vary depending on politics and secrecy.

---

# 8. Epic

Epic abilities are major national/international assets or threats depending on the ability.

Typical properties:
- extremely limited historical examples;
- major strategic value;
- high technique/evolution ceiling;
- ability to change battle, infrastructure, intelligence, logistics, or other high-level outcomes;
- intense institutional pressure around the user.

An Epic user's life may be shaped by the awakening event itself.

---

# 9. Super Epic

Super Epic abilities exceed ordinary strategic classifications.

Typical properties:
- extremely rare historical appearance;
- high-end rule interaction;
- exceptional growth potential;
- counters may be conditional rather than broadly available;
- individual users may influence major military or political planning.

The game should not introduce many Super Epic users casually.

---

# 10. Legendary

Legendary abilities are civilization-level historical classifications.

Typical properties:
- very few confirmed examples;
- associated with famous historical users/events;
- can alter the outcome of major conflicts;
- highly incomplete public knowledge;
- classified research and contingency planning likely exist.

Legendary does not mean invincible.

Every Legendary ability still requires:
- rules;
- costs;
- limitations;
- counters or failure conditions where coherent;
- a user capable of surviving/mastering it.

---

# 11. Prime Legendary

Prime Legendary abilities sit above ordinary Legendary classification.

They should represent abilities whose governing principle, potential, or historical effect is so exceptional that ordinary Legendary doctrine is insufficient.

Typical properties:
- civilization-defining strategic value;
- extremely low occurrence;
- incomplete understanding even among top institutions;
- unusual interaction with Level, stats, passives, or evolution;
- high likelihood of classified handling;
- possible cosmic/system significance.

Prime Legendary must remain genuinely rare in authored content.

---

# 12. Unique

A Unique ability is **1/1 in the universe** for that ability identity.

This is an ontological scarcity rule, not merely “the rarest drop rate.”

A Unique record must answer:
- why only one can exist;
- what happens if the user dies;
- whether the ability disappears, persists, transfers, sleeps, or can reappear;
- whether the Status recognizes uniqueness immediately;
- whether institutions know the truth;
- whether the cosmic system enforces exclusivity;
- whether copies, simulations, partial imitations, or derived techniques are possible.

Until a child record says otherwise, do not assume inheritance or respawn.

A Unique ability is not automatically stronger than every Prime Legendary in every situation. Its uniqueness may come from its governing law, role, or cosmic significance.

---

# 13. Awakening classification behavior

At the age-18 awakening event, Status identifies the primary ability and rarity.

Future documentation must distinguish:
- what the user sees;
- what school officials see;
- what the public announcement reveals;
- what automated records store;
- what can be classified/suppressed;
- what happens when rarity is misidentified or deliberately concealed.

Rare+ awakenings may trigger additional institutional procedures.

Exact thresholds for special treatment remain a social/world design task.

---

# 14. Rarity versus Level

Rarity and Level are independent axes.

Examples:
- a Level 40 Common user can be more dangerous than a Level 8 Epic user;
- a low-Level Legendary user may possess enormous potential but poor control;
- a high-Level Rare user can outperform higher-rarity opponents through mastery and passives.

Do not derive Level directly from rarity.

---

# 15. Rarity versus mastery

An ability's rarity does not grant mastered techniques automatically.

Users must still:
- practice;
- experiment;
- survive mistakes;
- learn;
- obtain knowledge;
- develop control;
- discover techniques;
- manage resource cost.

High-rarity abilities may be harder—not easier—to master.

---

# 16. Social consequences

Rarity may affect:
- school prestige;
- recruitment;
- scholarships/training access;
- government registration;
- military/security interest;
- research interest;
- media/public attention;
- discrimination;
- exploitation risk;
- protection;
- kidnapping/assassination threat;
- marriage/family politics where culturally relevant;
- faction recruitment.

These consequences belong to world/social authorities, not the rarity number alone.

---

# 17. Catalog authoring rule

Every ability catalog record must include:
- rarity;
- why that rarity is justified;
- comparison with adjacent rarity tiers;
- known historical frequency;
- expected technique/evolution ceiling;
- known drawbacks;
- known counters;
- classification confidence;
- whether public classification equals true classification.

No ability receives a rarity solely because it “sounds cool.”

---

# 18. Balance guardrails

Do not use rarity as an excuse for:
- limitless effects;
- no resource cost;
- no rules;
- arbitrary immunity;
- automatic victory;
- unrelated extra powers;
- ignoring Level/stats/skill;
- invalidating the tactical system.

Higher rarity increases design possibility and ceiling. It does not eliminate game rules.

---

# 19. Reconstruction acceptance

A future developer must be able to explain:
- what each rarity means;
- how rarity differs from Level;
- how rarity differs from mastery;
- why a Common can still be dangerous;
- why higher rarities are genuinely superior in potential;
- how Unique differs from merely very rare;
- what social/institutional consequences rarity can create.
