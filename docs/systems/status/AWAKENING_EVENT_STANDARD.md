# THE GAME — Age-18 Awakening & Public Classification Event

Status: **ACTIVE TARGET-GAME DESIGN**

Purpose: define the social/world structure around the universal human Status awakening at age 18.

## 1. Locked canon

Every human receives/activates the Status UI at age 18.

Schools gather for a public event where students are represented, identified, and classified.

The event is culturally and institutionally significant.

## 2. Event role

The awakening event simultaneously serves as:
- rite of passage;
- public classification;
- school prestige event;
- recruitment opportunity;
- social-status turning point;
- talent identification;
- government/institutional data collection point;
- major story event for young characters.

## 3. Required phases

Target event structure should include:

1. registration/identity verification;
2. school delegation arrival;
3. public opening;
4. individual or grouped awakening;
5. Status confirmation;
6. ability identification;
7. rarity classification;
8. medical/safety check;
9. official record creation;
10. public result presentation subject to privacy/classification rules;
11. recruitment/contact period;
12. post-awakening counseling/training assignment.

Exact ceremony and technology remain world-design tasks.

## 4. School representation

Schools may be evaluated socially by:
- number of students;
- average preparation;
- rare awakenings;
- student achievements;
- recruitment outcomes;
- historical alumni.

Schools do not control which primary ability a student receives unless later canon explicitly creates a preparation influence.

## 5. Rare awakenings

Rare+ results can trigger:
- security protocols;
- specialist evaluation;
- media attention;
- government interest;
- faction recruitment;
- scholarship/training offers;
- protective custody or classified handling in extreme cases.

Exact thresholds depend on rarity/social authority.

## 6. Unique awakening

A Unique result should be a major world event unless successfully classified/suppressed.

The event standard must support:
- immediate containment of information;
- conflicting institutional claims;
- security risk;
- verification that the result is truly Unique;
- public cover story if required.

## 7. Failure/anomaly cases

Future design must address:
- delayed awakening;
- no readable result;
- unstable manifestation;
- medical emergency;
- misclassification;
- concealed ability;
- multiple apparent signatures despite one-primary-ability rule;
- system malfunction versus misunderstood ability.

No anomaly may silently break the one-primary-ability canon.

## 8. Social consequences

Results may affect:
- education path;
- career prospects;
- military/security recruitment;
- research recruitment;
- family status;
- friendships/rivalries;
- discrimination;
- prestige;
- exploitation risk;
- criminal targeting.

## 9. Player experience

If Jack experiences this event in-game, it should be a full story sequence rather than a menu-only character creation screen.

Potential gameplay:
- preparation before age 18;
- school relationships;
- public pressure;
- rivals;
- ceremony choices;
- awakening reveal;
- reactions;
- immediate recruitment;
- private Status inspection;
- first training decisions.

Whether Jack starts exactly at this event remains a separate story decision.

## 10. Visual production

Required pixel-art families may include:
- event venue;
- school banners/insignia;
- student groups;
- awakening platform/device if one exists;
- Status manifestation FX;
- rarity presentation;
- officials;
- recruiters;
- crowd;
- security;
- portrait/reaction variants.

## 11. Open decisions

Still unresolved:
- event name;
- venue scale;
- whether events are local/regional/national;
- exact school age structure;
- whether awakening occurs automatically at birthday or is synchronized through the event;
- what technology/ritual mediates it;
- what fields are public;
- legal privacy;
- classified rarity handling;
- Jack's exact relationship to the ceremony.

## 12. Reconstruction acceptance

The final packet must let a future developer build the full event without guessing its purpose, phases, actors, data visibility, consequences, and art requirements.
